\section{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Few seeds, at a selected seed.} Most late effects in \cref{tab:levers,tab:negative} are single
    same-seed pairs at seed~73, which ranked \val{g7-s73-rank} of \val{g7-nseeds} in its sweep, and may not hold at
    other seeds. The row pool rests on one chip pair and one official pair from the same trajectory; the prewarm on
    one official pair and single-run step counts; the EMA blend on one chip pair. Our rule of three seeds below 0.0003
    was often not affordable.
  \item \textbf{Selection and the evaluation text.} Seeds and salts were chosen by rehearsal score (five of the seven
    final-day uploads carry a lottery-selected salt), and \upl{K65a}--\upl{K65d} were official tests of single
    changes. If the official shard overlaps the public validation text (\cref{sec:contest}), that selection used part
    of the evaluation set.
  \item \textbf{The offset may depend on the chip.} The frozen calibration's errors are positive on the faster
    final-day chips (\cref{tab:offset-chips}), where every lottery-selected final-day upload was rehearsed, so
    instance and selection are confounded; given the rehearsal score, the chip-G/H term is not significant
    (permutation $p=\val{cq-perm-p-adj}$). The oracle's headline statistics depend on one model-normalised rehearsal
    (\cref{tab:oracle-sens}). The pseudo-prospective analyses are retrospective; only three written projections are
    archived.
  \item \textbf{Determinism rests on little direct evidence.} One cold/warm pair with older code, and two replicate
    pairs that agree to the printed precision only until their first clock-triggered event
    (\cref{sec:determinism}). The within-chip offset SD
    (\val{off-sd-within}, 95\% CI \val{off-sd-within-lo}--\val{off-sd-within-hi}) contains the SD that re-drawing the
    order noise would give (about \val{noise-chip-pair}), so the offset statistics cannot tell a deterministic official run from one that re-draws the order noise. Step counts jitter by up to
    \val{jitter-range-hi} steps within a chip.
  \item \textbf{Hardware heterogeneity.} Instances differed by up to \val{chip-spread}\docnum{} in step time, and
    chip~D ran an older Neuron runtime (2.33.10, against 2.34.10 on chip~C). We normalised only same-recipe runs,
    never across a change that alters step time, and every comparison names its chip.
  \item \textbf{Released data do not cover the final day.} \texttt{runs.jsonl} holds chips A--D up to 29~September.
    The chip E, G and H runs behind the row-pool pair, the salt lottery, the EMA-blend and output-projection pairs, the
    pool-size ablation and the final-day test bench are quotes from campaign documents, marked \docnum{}
    (\cref{app:docfacts}): reported, not recomputable.
  \item \textbf{Development-set validation.} The surrogate's configuration was chosen among \val{sc-n}\docnum{}
    candidates with their LOPO results visible, and some feature encodings were set with campaign outcomes in view
    (\cref{sec:simval}), so LOPO and LOKVO may overstate how the model would do on fresh pairs. The \val{postfit-n}
    post-fit pairs are the only clean test, and there it is at chance.
  \item \textbf{Simulator circularity.} The surrogate was fitted on our own search trajectory, whose knobs are
    non-random and confounded with code eras (\cref{tab:coefficients}); some inputs come from a campaign ledger partly
    extracted from free text (\texttt{research/sim-data/dataset-qa.md}); its step prior is centred on the price it is
    sometimes used to support. On genuinely new mechanisms it is at chance (\val{postfit-sign}, \val{postfit-wilson}).
  \item \textbf{The price of a step.} It rests on a per-step rate whose number of comparisons is not recorded and on one
    long run, and it varies with the step count. A surrogate refit with a vague slope prior puts it at
    \val{kappa-vague-lo}--\val{kappa-vague-hi}, below the value we use (\cref{sec:price}). The gap figures
    extrapolate it, and other teams' recipes may sit on a different curve.
  \item \textbf{Survivor bias in the levers.} \Cref{tab:levers} lists the changes that were kept because they
    measured well, so their effects are biased upward, most for the single late pairs near the noise scale; the
    four campaign-log levers have no recorded comparison at all.
  \item \textbf{Uncertain noise scale.} The noise column rests on a salt SD quoted from a campaign document, and a
    lever that acts from step~0 faces the larger seed SD (\cref{sec:lever-notes}), so its ratio to the noise scale is an
    upper bound.
  \item \textbf{Leaderboard snapshot.} The references are from the last public snapshot we recorded (about
    \val{leaderboard-time}\docnum{}); the organisers' published final standings may differ.
  \item \textbf{Forking paths.} The campaign was adaptive and the analyses are post hoc. The spike threshold was
    chosen after seeing the data, and so were the start of the calibration window at \upl{K50}, the chip-C
    calibration set \upl{K51}--\upl{K60}, the burn-in of three uploads for the expanding window and the normalisation
    of \upl{K70a}'s rehearsal (\cref{sec:oracle-holdout}); the temporal and pseudo-prospective checks are least exposed
    to this.
  \item \textbf{Utilisation estimates.} ``About \val{mfu}\docnum{} MFU'' and ``\val{idle}\docnum{} idle'' are
    approximate profiles, used only to motivate the kernel hypothesis of \cref{sec:gap}.
\end{enumerate}
